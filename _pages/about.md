About
=====

I am a broadly trained geographer, landscape ecologist, and global change biologist interested in the relationships among ecosystem processes, land-use and land-cover change, and climate change. My research examines how terrestrial ecosystem function, structure, and composition respond to a changing climate and intensifying human activities. My work has addressed agricultural and forest land management, tropical forest responses to elevated CO₂, and spatiotemporal patterns of terrestrial carbon dynamics and plant biodiversity. Ultimately, my goal is to understand how climate change and human activities alter terrestrial ecosystems, ensuring the best available science informs environmental decision-making.

Currently, I am a Grand Challenges Postdoctoral Fellow at Chapman University. Here, I lead a NASA-funded project as PI to connect Chapman University and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory across five applications of hyperspectral and thermal remote sensing: plant biodiversity, coastal ecosystem health, terrestrial ecosystem function, urban heat, and wildfire. I am also a collaborator on ELEVATE, a NASA-funded scoping study led by Chapman University investigators Joshua B. Fisher and Gregory R. Goldsmith, that examines how tropical forests may respond to elevated atmospheric CO₂. As a Postdoctoral Fellow, I also instruct undergraduate courses in the Grand Challenges Initiative Program and lead in curriculum development and experiential learning.
